    The person on the radio

    A controller is somebody in a room, not a file being played. They take a moment after keying up, they are never quite the same loudness twice, and once in a while they say "uh".
    Breathing
    An audible breath in the gaps of a long clearance
    Weather behind them
    Wind gusting under the voice, the way an open tower cab sounds
    Hesitation
    The occasional "uh" before the callsign. Rare on purpose.

    Immersion

    The rest of the aeroplane. A first officer in the other seat and a cabin crew behind you, following the flight the same way the controller does. None of it goes out on the radio, and none of it changes what ATC says.
    First officer
    Somebody in the other seat
    Callouts, checklists and the numbers read back to you
    Their voice
    Standard callouts
    Eighty knots, V one, rotate, positive rate, and the calls on the way down
    Confirms clearances
    Says the numbers back to you on the interphone, so two of you heard them
    Checklists
    Called and answered at the points a flight actually runs them
    Works the radio for you
    Reads a clearance back if you have not, after a few seconds. Off unless you want it: the point of the radio is usually to work it yourself.
    Cabin
    Cabin crew
    A purser and a cabin, on the address system
    Their voice
    Safety briefing
    The demonstration during the taxi out, and the crew calls around it
    Announcements
    Welcome aboard, the cruise, the descent, and where you have arrived
    Food and drink service
    The trolley after the climb, and a call to the flight deck before it
    Cabin chime
    The two notes before an announcement, and the seatbelt sign
    Boarding music
    Plays quietly in the cabin while people get on, and stops at pushback
    Applause on a soft landing
    The cabin claps when you touch down gentler than 200 feet per minute
    Real crew voices
    A little interphone and cabin speaker on their voices, and the odd um and er
    Announcements in English too
    A crew whose language is not English repeat each cabin announcement in English
    Who they are, and how much
    Cabin volume
    80%
    How much they talk
    What the crew call the airline
    Left empty, they take it from your callsign, and say "this flight" for a private registration.
    Your captain's name
    Left empty, one is cast from the registration, and the same aeroplane keeps the same captain.
    Talk to the crew
    Say "cabin crew" or "ground crew" first, and they answer instead of the controller
    Recorded announcements
    Use your own recordings
    A real announcement beats a synthesised one. Where a recording exists for a moment, it is played instead.

    This is alpha software

    WilcoATC is in alpha. You will run into bugs, and you will run into a lot of them.

    The controller can say the wrong thing, or something that makes no sense at all.
    It can fail to understand you, or hear something you never said.
    Anything here can change or stop working from one version to the next.

    Flight

    The simulator supplies your position and radios. These are the details it cannot tell us.

    Getting the pieces

    Three things are too big to ship inside the program and are downloaded once: the voices your controllers speak with, the database of every airport and frequency, and the model that hears you. About 1.5 GB, and you only do this once.

    Diagnostics

    The same check wilcoatc doctor runs in a terminal: every part of the program, whether it is there, and what it found.
